1 · Your notes

Flicker as things speed up (42–56 s)

causeMostly deliberate, and it reads as a bug:

  • flicker() drops a room's light to 25% twice in 0.2 s, 16 times from 42.6 to 50.9 s. That's a strobe.
  • Camera shake runs the whole section (0.4, then 0.9 from 55.4 s). Sub-pixel jitter makes thin mullions and sills shimmer.
  • Hard cuts and whips about every second, plus up to 7 cards popping in.

fixRemove the strobe; signal panic with a slow lamp sway or one brownout dip, not blinking. Shake only for 0.3 s on each whip, never on a held shot. Fewer, longer cuts (about 1.5–2 s). Optional: TAA/FXAA so moving mullions stay clean.

Deck agent's arm "makes no sense"

causeThe show pose raises one arm straight forward while the agent stands with its back to us, against the monitor. It holds for 1.2 s, then snaps back. Poses blend Euler angles per bone, so the arm swings on an odd arc on the way.

fixA real presenter beat:

  • Step back from the desk and turn three-quarters to the window.
  • The finished slide appears on the wall screen or whiteboard behind them.
  • An open-palm "ta-da" with anticipation and follow-through.
  • Quaternion (slerp) blending for every pose change, so arms never take shortcuts through the body.

Showing the phone

causeThe iOS agent holds out an empty hand; there's no phone prop. The arm goes straight out at waist height.

proposalThe beat you described, borrowing the film's lens POV:

  • Pick the iPhone up off the desk, walk to the window, and hold it up to the glass, screen out.
  • Cut to the watcher's 25× view: push in on that phone until lamps.app fills the frame (oval lens vignette).
  • "It's built. Try it on your phone" arrives, and the watcher's own phone opens lamps.app.

Phone screens look unfinished

measured

  • Dynamic Island 31 pt too low. The model puts it 9.2 mm below the top of the display; on a real iPhone it's about 4.5 mm (11–48 pt). It sits on the header: it hides the thread title ("Cleanup") and the top of banners.
  • Low resolution. The UI canvas is 590×1280 (1.34 px per point). Presented on a Retina screen the phone covers about 1,600 device pixels, so text is upscaled and soft.
  • Blank peek. The conversation behind the Chats sidebar is empty rounded rectangles.
  • Framing. The phone's size and position change by shot. It overlaps the viewfinder corners and sometimes the frame edge.
  • The glass is flat: no reflection, so it reads as a sticker.

fix

  • Move the island to the iOS position.
  • Lay out with the real safe area: status bar 0–54 pt, content below.
  • Draw at 2× (1180×2560) with mipmaps.
  • Real messages in the peek.
  • One "present" framing: phone 78% of frame height, centred right, viewfinder UI hidden.
  • A faint glass reflection and edge highlight.

Game → Lamp Factory

proposalThe gamer is building a cosy isometric factory game.

  • Gameplay: lamp bases ride a conveyor past stations (stem, shade, bulb) and light up as they finish, then get boxed. A counter reads "Lamps built: 1,204".
  • Agent pane: "Add a shade-painting station."
  • Frantic sign: "MAKE 400 MORE?"
  • Why: it ties into lamps.app and the 400-lamp order, and isometric 2D looks far better at this size than fake 3D.

Lamp Easter eggs

  • Desk lamps: every room gets a different lamp from the lamps.app catalogue: brass, mushroom, arc, lava, paper lantern, banker's.
  • Mid-film: a "LAMPS" box drops in the shopper's room. At the end, after "Order 400?" is approved, a stack of 400 boxes.
  • Final blackout: every window goes dark except the shopper's, glowing with 400 lamps. A last laugh before the logo.
  • Other jokes: the watcher's own lamp is the brass one ("Arrives Friday"), a courtyard gas lamp, and a "Lamp of the month" poster.

2 · Other things that look off

The facade is too clean and uniform

One white wall, one window type, repeated: it's the most "CG" thing on the page. The film's courtyard is a patchwork: brick and stucco at different heights, a roof garden, an awning, a skyline in layers behind.

fix

  • Two or three building masses with different materials (brick, painted stucco), floor heights and window styles.
  • A sliver of skyline and water towers behind, and a lane or alley glimpse.
  • Soft dirt and weathering on the sills.

Room shots: empty walls, subject at the edge, a blurry slab

  • Close room shots put the agent at the right edge and leave half the frame as blank wall.
  • A huge out-of-focus white sill runs across the bottom.
  • Rooms have one poster and little else.

fix

  • Frame each agent on a third.
  • Lower the sill or keep it sharp.
  • Give each room a personality like the film's tenants: books, a record player, a cat bed, plants, wall art, a lamp.

The watcher's reaction shots

Ours is wide, in a bare room against a flat blue wall: it reads as a stage set. The film cuts close: face and hands, warm practical light, depth behind.

fix

  • Shoot the reverse through his own window (sill, curtain edge, glass reflection) so he's the one being watched.
  • Go tighter on the head, hands and phone, with the phone glow lighting him.
  • Dress the room behind him: shelves, framed photos, the brass lamp.

Daytime flattens everything

At midday the facade washes out, room lights don't read, and the "lights out, blinds down" ending barely registers.

fix

  • Keep your time-of-day lighting, but floor it at late afternoon or golden hour.
  • Always show interior light, with stronger contrast and warm windows against a cooler facade.
  • Night stays as it is; it's closest to the film.

Motion feels robotic

  • Every pose change is a straight blend between two Euler poses, with no anticipation, overshoot or weight shift.
  • Idle agents stand perfectly still apart from breathing.

fix

  • Slerp blending plus small secondary motion: head look-arounds, weight shifts, a sip of coffee, a stretch.
  • The cheapest big win: it makes the courtyard feel alive between beats.
